## Transcript

Leo used to begin every study session with the hardest chapter because he believed that discipline meant facing the worst task first. The plan sounded sensible, but on busy days he often delayed starting at all. He changed one part of the routine: for the first ten minutes, he now reviews a familiar example connected to the difficult topic. This short start does not replace serious practice. Instead, it helps him recall useful ideas and makes the next step clearer. After two weeks, Leo was beginning earlier and completing more of the difficult chapter. The improvement came from reducing the cost of starting, not from making the main task easier.

## Frozen training tasks

1. Explain why Leo's original plan often failed.
2. Describe the one change he made.
3. Distinguish the warm-up from avoiding difficult work.
4. Give a short recommendation to a student with the same problem.

## Scoring range

- Cause: the intimidating start increased delay.
- Change: ten minutes with a familiar, connected example.
- Contrast: the warm-up prepares the main work; it does not replace or simplify it.
